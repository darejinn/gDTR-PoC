% ABSTRACT
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{abstract}
Genomic foundation models excel at sequence prediction, yet \emph{where} in their layer hierarchy biological structure is actually recognised remains opaque. We introduce \gDTR{} (\emph{Genomic Deep-Thinking Ratio}), a training-free framework that pinpoints, for every nucleotide, the layer at which the model's intermediate prediction stabilises on its final answer -- its \emph{settling depth}. Applied to Evo~2~7B, \gDTR{} recovers a coherent ordering of biological elements: well-defined splice donors are recognised earliest, regulatory enhancers slightly deeper, coding exons deeper still, and introns and 5$'$~UTRs deepest. The ordering is not an artefact of prediction difficulty -- it survives partial-correlation control against per-token entropy -- and causal edits to the canonical GT splice motif and to its dinucleotide-shuffled flank both shift settling depth in the predicted direction, showing that motif strength and flanking grammar jointly drive the depth. The same readout, applied to thousands of ClinVar variants, separates molecular-consequence classes by the depth at which a single substitution maximally disrupts the residual stream. \gDTR{} thus complements attention-, dictionary-, and output-side interpretability tools with a fourth, hierarchical axis: \emph{where, in the layer stack, biology is recognised}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Genomic causal foundation models such as Evo~2 \cite{nguyen2026evo2}, HyenaDNA~\cite{nguyen2023hyenadna}, the Nucleotide Transformer~\cite{dallatorre2024nt}, DNABERT-2~\cite{zhou2024dnabert2}, and Caduceus~\cite{schiff2024caduceus} have catalysed a paradigm shift in functional genomics. Yet research into their internal mechanisms still lags: layer-wise computational dynamics remain largely opaque, leaving open whether model outputs are grounded in biology that a human geneticist would recognise. Existing interpretability tools probe \emph{where} models attend~\cite{abnar2020rollout,avsec2021enformer}, \emph{what} they encode~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse}, or \emph{how} variants shift representations~\cite{cheng2023alphamissense,jaganathan2019spliceai}. None addresses \emph{where in the layer hierarchy} a biological element is recognised.

We introduce a fourth, hierarchical axis. The Deep-Thinking Ratio of~\citet{chen2026deepthinking} measures, in NLP transformers, the layer index at which intermediate logit-lens distributions~\cite{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,belrose2023tunedlens,pal2023futurelens} converge within $\varepsilon$ of the final layer; the construct echoes the broader observation that mid-stack layers carry interpretable factual content~\cite{geva2022ffn,meng2022rome}. We adapt this depth-of-convergence machinery to genomic causal LMs, but note an important conceptual asymmetry: genomic input positions are not ``thinking'', so we re-cast the readout as \emph{representational stabilisation depth}, which we hypothesise tracks the lexical strength of biological motifs. Three adaptations are required: a cosine unembed-residual lens to escape JSD saturation in the $|V|\!\le\!512$ vocabulary; a running-minimum envelope to tame architecture-specific non-monotonicity (StripedHyena~\cite{poli2023striped}, pure Hyena~\cite{poli2023hyena}, Transformer-MLM~\cite{vaswani2017attention}); and calibration against the post-final-norm reference to handle Evo~2's idle final block (App.~\ref{app:architectural-quirk}; $L^\star\!=\!29$).

Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic} summarises the readout. The remainder of this paper tests, on Evo~2~7B and three peer genomic foundation models, the central hypothesis: \emph{biologically salient sequence elements stabilise at characteristically shallow layers, and this depth ordering tracks lexical motif strength.}

% 2. FRAMEWORK

\section{The \gDTR{} Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

For a nucleotide sequence of length $T$ processed by a causal language model with $L$ layers, we extract the residual-stream activation $\hl(t)$ at every layer $\ell\!\in\!\{1,\ldots,L\}$ and token position $t$. We replace the JSD lens of the parent DTR~\cite{chen2026deepthinking} with a cosine unembed-residual lens that operates directly in hidden-state geometry,
\begin{equation}
  \Dcos(\ell, t) \;=\; 1 - \cos\!\bigl(\hl(t),\, \hnorm(t)\bigr),
  \label{eq:cos-lens}
\end{equation}
where $\hnorm(t)$ is the post-final-norm reference. Whereas NLP transformers exhibit near-monotonic logit-lens convergence~\cite{nostalgebraist2020logitlens,belrose2023tunedlens}, genomic CLMs do not (Hyena alignment rotations, Evo~2 idle final block). We therefore define the \emph{settling depth} via a running-minimum envelope,
\begin{equation}
  c(t) \;=\; \min\bigl\{\, \ell \;:\; \runmin\,\Dcos(\ell, t) \le \gamma_{\cos}\bigr\},
  \label{eq:settling}
\end{equation}
with $\runmin\,\Dcos(\ell,t) = \min_{k\le\ell}\Dcos(k,t)$. The threshold $\gamma_{\cos}$ is set by \emph{regional $q_{70}$ calibration}: the 70th percentile of the running-min cosine-distance at the penultimate layer over the analysis region. The 70th percentile sits inside a flat plateau of a $5\!\times\!5$ grid sweep over $(\gamma_{\cos},\rho)$ (App.~\ref{app:gamma-sweep}; SD across 25 cells $<0.04$). The original deep-thinking ratio $\mathrm{DTR}(t)\!=\!\mathbf{1}\{c(t) \!>\! \rho L\}$ follows directly with $\rho\!=\!0.85$.

As a diagnostic that the residual-stream taps used by \gDTR{} carry information linearly recoverable to the post-final-norm reference, a per-layer tuned-lens affine fit~\cite{belrose2023tunedlens} reaches $\ge 98\%$ MSE recovery on $30/32$ Evo~2 layers (canonical $L^\star\!=\!29$: $0.9996$; App.~\ref{app:tuned-lens}); this establishes that the raw-tap \gDTR{} readout is not operating on degenerate representations. The framework itself remains training-free, as no affine weights are used at inference.

\section{Biological Utility}
\label{sec:utility}

\subsection{Hierarchy of biological depth}
\label{sec:shallowness}

We first test whether $c(t)$ delivers a biologically meaningful, transferable readout at the genome scale. Chromosome~22 serves as the calibration set ($\gamma_{\cos}\!=\!0.397$ from $q_{70}$ at the penultimate layer); chromosome~17 is held out for validation with that $\gamma$ frozen. The chr17 $q_{70}$ recomputed independently equals $0.394$ ($|\Delta|\!=\!0.0023$); the chr17 splice-donor effect reaches $94.6\%$ of the chr22 magnitude (Cohen's $d\!=\!-0.349$ vs intron). The calibration transfers without re-tuning.

\textbf{An ordered hierarchy.} On the seven canonical genomic contexts (Fig.~\ref{fig:shallowness}a), splice donor and acceptor sites converge $\sim 2$ layers earlier than the intronic baseline, while non-splice contexts cluster at or above the baseline. The effect is monotone in donor/acceptor magnitude and statistically significant at every context (Mann--Whitney $p\!<\!10^{-4}$). The shallowness axis extends to ENCODE cCRE-ELS enhancer-like elements~\cite{moore2020encode}: on chr22, cCRE-ELS positions are shallower than a per-position background by Cohen's $d\!=\!-0.190$ ($p_{\mathrm{Bonf}}\!<\!10^{-4}$), against a splice-donor reference of $-0.433$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:shallowness}b). The full hierarchy reads \emph{splice donor $<$ cCRE-ELS $<$ exon $<$ intron $<$ 5$'$~UTR} (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}b). GTEx eQTLs~\cite{gtex2020} ($d\!=\!-0.022$) and GWAS Catalog SNPs~\cite{sollis2023gwas} ($d\!=\!-0.040$) sit on the same axis at biologically marginal magnitudes; their formal significance follows from the very large $n$. The within-splice motif breakdown (canonical GT-AG, canonical GC-AG, non-canonical) reverses the naive ``stronger motif $\Rightarrow$ shallower'' prior~\cite{wang2008splicecode,yeo2004maxentscan,burge1998u12} and is reported in App.~\ref{app:splice-canonical}.

\textbf{The hierarchy is not entropy artefact.} A natural concern is that $c(t)$ merely tracks the per-position next-token Shannon entropy $H_t$ of the model. We forward $120$ random chr22 windows ($720{,}000$ positions), compute $H_t$ from the post-norm logits, and find an overall Spearman $\rho(c, H_t)\!=\!-0.079$ (per-context $|\rho|\!\le\!0.15$ except $5'$~UTR; \S\ref{sec:discussion}). When we regress $c$ on $H_t$ and recompute the splice-donor-versus-intron Cohen's $d$ on the residual, the effect strengthens from $-0.452$ to $-0.583$: the depth ordering is not an entropy artefact.

\textbf{The hierarchy tracks lexical motif strength.} Within the splice-donor class, we ask which sequence component carries the shallowness. On $1{,}000$ canonical GT-AG donors (chr22, $\pm 10$~bp pad-averaged $c$, matching the convention used in Fig.~\ref{fig:shallowness}a), real donors settle at $\bar c\!=\!26.77$. Replacing the central GT with AA (preserving the entire $\pm 100$~bp flank) deepens $\bar c$ by $0.46$ layers (paired Wilcoxon $p\!=\!2.3\!\times\!10^{-32}$, Cohen's $d\!=\!-0.09$): a small but highly reliable contribution of the canonical dinucleotide to the shallow-recognition signal. Conversely, dinucleotide-shuffling the $\pm 100$~bp flank while preserving the central GT shallows $\bar c$ by $3.18$ layers (paired $p\!=\!4.1\!\times\!10^{-59}$, $d\!=\!+0.51$). The shuffled flank lets the GT pop out in isolation, while the real flank engages deeper splice-grammar integration; both motif and flanking context jointly determine the canonical depth.

\subsection{Variant disruption as a depth probe}
\label{sec:variant-disruption}

If $c(t)$ tracks lexical motif strength on \emph{healthy} sequence, the per-variant argmax-layer of $|\DDcos|$ should reveal the depth at which a single substitution disrupts an already-recognised motif -- a depth probe, not a scorer. We test this on the full ClinVar~\cite{landrum2020clinvar} 15-cancer-gene cohort: $10{,}910$ SNVs (cached features) augmented by $1{,}064$ indels forwarded with the same Evo~2~7B pipeline (the SNV cache had silently filtered indels in the original Phase~3 run). Variants are class-stratified using ClinVar \texttt{MC} priorities (canonical-splice $\succ$ nonsense $\succ$ frameshift $\succ$ missense $\succ$ synonymous $\succ$ intron).

Argmax-layer medians order \emph{intron $<$ frameshift $<$ nonsense $<$ missense $\approx$ canonical splice $<$ synonymous} (Fig.~\ref{fig:variants}, top), differing across the six classes at Kruskal--Wallis $p\!=\!3.0\!\times\!10^{-10}$~\cite{kruskal1952wallis}. Adjacent-pair Dunn--Bonferroni tests~\cite{mann1947whitney,benjamini1995fdr} highlight the largest jump as nonsense $\to$ missense ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}\!<\!10^{-3}$); the remaining adjacent gaps fall within noise at these sample sizes ($n\!=\!32$--$1{,}740$). Mechanistically, early-truncation events (frameshift, nonsense) disrupt the residual stream shallowly, whereas perturbations of already-established protein-level features (missense, splice motif) peak deeper. Five representative $\DDcos$ traces, one per non-intron class at the per-class median argmax-layer, are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:variants}, bottom. The variant labelled ``frameshift locus'' in earlier drafts (\emph{BRCA1} 17:43{,}057{,}063 G$\to$A) is in fact a nonsense SNV (BRCA1 W1837X) and is now correctly classed.

As a sanity check on the trajectory's information content (and not as a scorer claim), a logistic regression on the full 32-dimensional $\DDcos$ trajectory reaches 10-fold stratified AUROC $0.844$ on the $8{,}008$ ClinVar P/LP-vs-B/LB cohort, against $0.729$ for the best single-layer tap; full per-feature numbers, DeLong~\cite{delong1988comparing} pairwise tests, and the per-layer ablation are deferred to App.~\ref{app:auroc-detail} and App.~\ref{app:per-layer}.


\subsection{Architecture dependence as a robustness check}
\label{sec:cross-arch}

Extending the chr22 windows to four genomic foundation models with divergent backbones (Evo~2~7B, HyenaDNA-large, NT-v2~500M, DNABERT-2; App.~\ref{app:fm-specs}) reveals not a universal invariance but a tokenisation-gated, two-tier structure (Tab.~\ref{tab:cross-arch-summary}). Within the per-position causal-LM family the pairwise Spearman $\rho$ of mean settling depth is $+0.516$ (moderate concordance, given the very different layer counts of $32$ vs $8$) and the donor $<$ intron inequality replicates at depth-normalised scale; within the per-window MLM family $\rho\!=\!+0.663$ but the splice-junction signal averages out across BPE/$k$-mer tokens. Cross-family correlations are weakly negative ($-0.12$ to $-0.29$). \emph{Tokenisation granularity}, not architecture per se, gates the readout.

% 4. DISCUSSION
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three caveats temper the hierarchy claim. First, $c(t)$ \emph{partially} confounds with prediction entropy at $5'$~UTR (Spearman $\rho\!=\!+0.41$, against $|\rho|\!\le\!0.15$ at all other contexts); the apparent depth of $5'$~UTR ($\bar c\!=\!29.22$) therefore mixes genuine deep-integration of UTR structure (uORFs, IRES) with model uncertainty. The hierarchy claim should be read as restricted to contexts whose $|\rho(c, H_t)|$ is small. Second, the cosine UR lens is calibrated against the post-final-norm reference; per-layer fidelity is best in the deep-layer regime where the linear unembed is well-conditioned (App.~\ref{app:tuned-lens}), and weaker for very shallow layers. Reported settling depths in the $\ell\!\le\!10$ band should therefore be interpreted with caution. Third, the cross-architecture comparison conflates architectural family (causal LM vs.\ MLM) with readout granularity (per-position vs.\ per-window); a same-tokenisation comparison of architectures, deferred to future work, is needed to disentangle the two.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% 5. CONCLUSION
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\gDTR{} establishes a training-free, layer-resolved interpretability axis for genomic causal language models, organised around the testable hypothesis that biologically salient sequence elements stabilise at characteristically shallow layers. Per-token settling depth surfaces a coherent hierarchy (splice donor $<$ cCRE-ELS $<$ exon $<$ intron $<$ 5$'$~UTR), survives partial-correlation control against prediction entropy ($d_{\mathrm{splice\,vs\,intron}}$ strengthens from $-0.452$ to $-0.583$), tracks lexical motif strength (destroying the canonical GT shifts $\bar c$ by $0.46$ layers, paired $p\!=\!2.3\!\times\!10^{-32}$), and yields a class-stratified disruption layer across $4{,}023$ ClinVar P/LP variants ($p\!=\!3\!\times\!10^{-10}$). Cross-architecture validation refines invariance to a tokenisation-gated, two-tier property. Whole-genome scaling, integration with sparse-dictionary~\cite{cunningham2023sparse} and causal-edit~\cite{meng2022rome} methods, and population-genomics extensions~\cite{karczewski2020gnomad} are left to future work.

\section{Splice donor / acceptor positional fine-profile}
\label{app:splice-fine}

On chr17 the donor profile reaches its mean settling-depth minimum at
position $+20$\,bp ($\bar c = 24.06$) and remains $\ge 1.5$ layers
below intronic baseline ($\bar c = 27.69$) out to $\pm 200$\,bp. On
the same chromosome the acceptor profile reaches its minimum at
$+50$\,bp ($\bar c = 23.33$) and similarly remains depressed beyond
$\pm 200$\,bp. On chr22 both donor and acceptor minima sit at coordinate
$0$ ($\bar c = 23.65$ and $23.64$ respectively). The asymmetry --- donor
minimum exonic-side, acceptor minimum intronic-side --- mirrors the
asymmetric splice-grammar features (branch point, polypyrimidine tract)
that lie predominantly on the intronic side of acceptors.

\section{Canonical vs.\ non-canonical splice motif breakdown}
\label{app:splice-canonical}

A finer dissection of the splice axis by motif class -- using the
genomic dinucleotides immediately downstream of the donor and upstream
of the acceptor -- reveals an unexpected ordering: \emph{non-canonical}
splice donors (AT-AC and other rare motifs, pooled $\bar c\!=\!25.13$,
$n\!=\!5{,}977$) converge \emph{earlier} than the dominant canonical
GT-AG donors ($\bar c\!=\!25.79$, $n\!=\!350{,}552$), while the minor
canonical GC-AG class is the deepest of the three
($\bar c\!=\!27.01$, $n\!=\!5{,}165$). The universality vs.\ intron
($\bar c\!=\!27.72$) holds for every splice class, but the within-class
ordering is the opposite of a naive ``stronger motif $\Rightarrow$
shallower recognition'' prior. The Cohen's $d$ between canonical GT-AG
and non-canonical donors is small ($\approx 0.05$), consistent with the
constrained branch-point and polypyrimidine context that flanks
non-canonical introns~\cite{wang2008splicecode,burge1998u12}; we
report the magnitudes honestly rather than over-interpret. The finding
replicates qualitatively on the 8-layer HyenaDNA-large at depth-normalised
scale.
